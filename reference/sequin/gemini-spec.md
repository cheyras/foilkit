It is easily distinguished from smooth, continuous sheens (like standard base set holos), scattered distinct shapes (like the stars and orbs in Cosmos foil), or rigid geometric grids (like cracked ice or crosshatch). The defining characteristic is the localized "popping" or glittering of individual, tightly packed cells.

## Shader Notes
*   **Cellular Noise:** Use a Voronoi or cellular noise function to generate the base pattern of densely packed, irregular "sequin" shapes.
*   **Independent Normals:** Assign a randomized, slightly perturbed normal vector to each individual cell generated by the noise function. This ensures they catch the light at different angles.
*   **View Angle Calculation:** Calculate the dot product between the view vector (driven by the tilt uniform) and the randomized normal of each cell.
*   **Sharp Thresholding:** Apply a sharp step function or high-power curve to the dot product result. This creates the abrupt "on/off" popping effect rather than a smooth fade.
*   **Iridescent Color Mapping:** Map the resulting intensity to a rainbow color ramp (gradient texture or procedural HSV shift) to simulate the holographic color separation.
*   **Masking:** The entire effect must be masked using a texture to only appear in the designated background areas of the art window, leaving the character and borders unaffected.
