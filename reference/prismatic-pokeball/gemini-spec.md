## Static Appearance
The foil pattern is characterized by a large, distinct Pokeball watermark embedded within a dense mosaic of irregular, geometric polygons (resembling shattered glass or a Voronoi diagram). 
*   **Scale**: The central Pokeball spans roughly half the width of the card's text box. The individual polygonal cells are much smaller, about 1/15th to 1/10th of the card's width.
*   **Colors**: The foil reflects a vibrant, full-spectrum rainbow (red, orange, yellow, green, blue, purple).
*   **Location**: On the *Professor's Research* card, the foil is applied as a "reverse holo," covering the lower text box and the background of the upper art window, while the character (Professor Elm) and the card borders remain opaque and non-reflective.

## Tilt Animation
As the card is tilted under the light source (observed across frames 1-7):
*   **Color Shifting**: The primary animation is a hue rotation. The colors sweep across the card in broad gradients. For example, the center of the text box shifts from orange/yellow (Frame 1) to green (Frame 2), back to orange/red (Frame 3), and eventually to deep blue/purple (Frame 7).
*   **Faceted Reflection**: The polygonal cells do not move or change shape. Instead, they act as discrete, flat mirrors. Because each cell appears to have a slightly different "tilt" or normal angle, adjacent cells sample slightly different colors from the underlying rainbow gradient. This creates a sharp, faceted, prismatic twinkling effect rather than a smooth, continuous gradient.
*   **Pokeball Visibility**: The large Pokeball outline remains static in position but catches the light and shifts colors in tandem with the surrounding mosaic.

## Layer Structure Hypothesis
*   **Base Foil**: A reflective metallic layer.
*   **Embossing/Texture**: The foil layer is stamped or etched with the polygonal mosaic and the Pokeball design. This physical texture alters the surface normals of each cell, causing them to reflect light at different angles.
*   **Ink Layer**: Opaque CMYK ink is printed over the foil to create the non-holographic elements (the character, text, and borders). The narration at 20:20-20:56 describes two separate embossments, the second applied over the ink, which suggests a specialized top-coat or secondary stamping step that solvent can strip — though visually the pattern behaves like a standard textured foil beneath the ink.

## Distinguishing Features
*   This pattern is easily identified by the combination of the large, recognizable Pokeball motif and the sharp, geometric "shattered glass" mosaic.
*   It differs from smooth rainbow foils by having discrete, hard-edged cells that break up the color gradient.
*   It differs from "starlight" or "cosmic" foils by using contiguous polygons rather than isolated dots or stars.

## Shader Notes
*   **Mosaic Generation**: Use a Voronoi or cell noise function to generate the polygonal grid.
*   **Normal Mapping**: Assign a slightly randomized normal vector (or a scalar tilt offset) to each discrete cell generated by the noise function.
*   **Pokeball Overlay**: Use a signed distance field (SDF) or a texture mask to draw the Pokeball outline, giving it a distinct normal offset so it stands out from the background mosaic.
*   **Iridescence**: Calculate the dot product of the view vector and the modified normal of each cell. Use this value to sample a 1D rainbow gradient texture (hue ramp).
*   **Masking**: Multiply the final foil output by a grayscale mask texture to ensure the effect only appears in the designated reverse holo areas (text box and art background).
